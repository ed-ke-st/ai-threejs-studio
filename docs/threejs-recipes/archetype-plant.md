# Building Potted Plants and Flowers

Small plants and flowers are tabletop-scale grouped objects: a container, soil, stems, and leaves or petals. They differ from trees by their small scale and their pot.

## Parts and grouping

Potted plant: a pot (a tapered cylinder, wider at the top), soil (a short dark cylinder set inside the pot rim), and foliage — several leaves (flattened, scaled spheres or cones) or stems (thin cylinders) rising from the soil. Flower: a thin stem (cylinder), a centre (small sphere), and petals (flattened scaled spheres or thin boxes arranged radially around the centre). Group several flowers into a bunch or bouquet.

## Proportions

Treat 1 unit as roughly 1 metre, so a potted plant is small — a pot about 0.12-0.2m across and the plant 0.3-0.6m tall. Rest the pot on the surface and grow foliage upward from the soil.

## Materials and style

Pot: terracotta (a warm orange-brown) or ceramic, matte to slightly glossy. Soil: dark brown, high roughness. Leaves and petals: saturated greens and flower colours, high roughness, no metalness, and flatShading for a stylized look. Use a small emissive only for glowing or magical plants.

## Common failure modes

A plant the size of a tree — keep it small and potted. Foliage floating above the pot — start stems and leaves at the soil. A single green blob — separate the pot, soil, and individual leaves or petals into a group.
