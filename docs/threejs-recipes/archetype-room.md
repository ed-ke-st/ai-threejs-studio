# Building Interior Rooms

An interior room is built from a floor, two or three walls, and furniture grouped by object. Leave the front open toward the camera so the interior is visible, and keep everything to human scale.

## Structure

Use a wide thin box for the floor. Build walls as thin boxes standing on the floor — typically a back wall and one side wall forming an L, not a full enclosing box, so the camera can see inside. Walls are about 2.5-3m tall. Add a ceiling only if the shot looks down into the room.

## Proportions and layout

Treat 1 unit as roughly 1 metre. A small room is about 4-6m across. Place furniture against the walls with walking space between pieces and do not overlap objects. A chair seat is about 0.45m high, a table or desk about 0.75m, a sofa seat about 0.45m.

## Furniture

Build each furniture piece as its own named group (see the furniture recipe): bed, desk, chair, shelf, sofa, rug. Group multi-part props so each can be moved as a unit.

## Materials and lighting

Use warm wood and muted wall colours and vary roughness. Light with a soft ambient or hemisphere fill plus a directional key coming through a window direction; add warm point or emissive lamps for cosy interiors. Frame the camera looking into the open corner of the room.

## Common failure modes

A fully enclosed box hides the interior — leave the front, and often one side, open. Furniture floating or intersecting walls — rest pieces on the floor and leave gaps between them.
