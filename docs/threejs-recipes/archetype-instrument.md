# Building Musical Instruments

Instruments are recognisable from a few characteristic parts. Build each as a named group and get the silhouette right.

## Common instruments

Guitar: a flat rounded body (a wide flattened cylinder, or a capsule lying flat), a long thin neck (a box) with a headstock (a small box) at the end, and thin strings (very thin long boxes) running along the neck. Piano or keyboard: a box body with a row of thin white key boxes and narrower black key boxes on top; a grand piano adds a curved lid and three legs. Drum: a cylinder shell with thin disc heads top and bottom, on a stand. Trumpet or horn: a cylinder tube ending in a cone bell. Violin: like a small guitar with a scroll and a separate bow (a thin stick).

## Proportions and grouping

Group the instrument and name the parts (Body, Neck, Headstock, Strings). Keep a guitar about 1m long and a piano keyboard about 1.2m wide. Space keys and strings evenly.

## Materials

Wood bodies: warm brown, medium roughness, no metalness, sometimes a glossy finish (lower roughness). Metal parts — strings, hardware, brass instruments — are metallic. Piano keys: white and near-black, low roughness.

## Common failure modes

An unrecognisable blob — capture the characteristic silhouette (a guitar body plus a long neck, a row of piano keys). Missing strings or keys — add them as thin repeated boxes.
