# Building Spaceships and Rockets

Spacecraft are grouped objects: a main hull, a nose or cockpit, wings or fins, engines with emissive exhaust, and glowing detail lights. Build the whole craft as one named group and orient it deliberately.

## Parts and grouping

Rocket: a tall cylinder hull, a cone nose on top, three or four symmetric fins around the base, a nozzle and an emissive exhaust flame (a downward-pointing cone) at the bottom, and a porthole (an emissive sphere). Spaceship or shuttle: a horizontal hull (a capsule or stretched box), a cockpit (a smaller box or half sphere), swept wings (boxes or thin wedges), and engine pods at the back with emissive thrusters.

## Symmetry and orientation

Mirror left/right wings and fins across the centre. Point a rocket up (long axis along Y); point a ship forward (long axis along Z or X). Place an engine glow point light at the thrusters.

## Materials and lighting

Hull: metallic — low-to-medium roughness, higher metalness — in white or grey with a coloured accent (red, blue). Thrusters and engines: bright emissive (orange or blue) with a matching point light. Windows and portholes: emissive blue. Use a dark space background, and add a few small emissive spheres as distant stars if a space scene is wanted.

## Common failure modes

A single tube with no nose, fins, or engine — split into hull, nose, fins, nozzle, and flame. Asymmetric fins or wings — mirror them across the centre. No glow — add emissive thrusters plus a matching point light.
