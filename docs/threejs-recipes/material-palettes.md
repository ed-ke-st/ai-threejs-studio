# Material Palettes

Material choices should support form and hierarchy. Most generated scenes improve when the palette is small and roughness variation carries more of the visual interest than color count.

## Neutral Base Pattern

Use two or three restrained base materials for walls, floors, and large structural forms. Small accent objects can carry stronger color. This prevents the whole scene from competing at once.

## Roughness Strategy

Vary roughness before adding more hues. Matte walls, semi-gloss props, and selective metallic accents usually create enough separation for readable lighting.

## Metalness Strategy

Reserve higher metalness for hardware, trim, or intentional hero materials. If too many surfaces are metallic, the scene can become visually noisy and hard to light.

## Contrast Strategy

Maintain value contrast between the main subject and the background. If the object is dark, the background or lighting must lift separation. If the object is bright, keep the background slightly quieter.

## Failure Modes

Scenes flatten when every object shares the same color family, roughness value, and brightness range. They also become chaotic when every object gets a saturated accent color.
