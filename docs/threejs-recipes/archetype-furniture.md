# Building Furniture

Furniture is the canonical multi-part group: a few simple boxes and cylinders, named and assembled to human proportions, resting on the floor. Always build each piece as one named group so it moves and scales as a unit.

## Chair

Group named "Chair" containing a seat (a box about 0.45m high), a backrest (a thin box rising behind the seat to about 0.9m), and four legs (thin cylinders or boxes at the corners, running from the floor up to the seat). The seat is about 0.45-0.5m square.

## Table and desk

Group named "Table" containing a tabletop (a flat box or cylinder at about 0.75m height) and legs — either four corner legs, or a central column plus a base foot for a round side table. Desks add a back panel or drawers.

## Shelf and cabinet

Group containing side panels, a back panel, and several horizontal shelves (thin boxes) spaced at intervals.

## Proportions and materials

Treat 1 unit as roughly 1 metre and keep heights realistic: seat 0.45m, table 0.75m, shelf depth about 0.3m. Wood uses medium-high roughness, no metalness, and brown tones; metal legs use lower roughness, some metalness, and a dark colour. Rest the bottoms of all legs at y=0.

## Common failure modes

A single box standing in for a chair or table — split it into seat/top plus legs plus back. Legs not reaching the floor, or the whole piece floating. Cloned identical pieces — vary materials and dimensions.
