# Building Food and Small Props

Food items are small, tabletop-scale props built from rounded primitives, usually presented on a plate or board. Build each item as a named group and keep the scale small and consistent with a surface.

## Common items

A cake or burger is a stack of layered cylinders (sponge/patty, filling, bun) with toppings (small spheres) on top. A piece of fruit is a sphere or capsule with a small stem (a thin cylinder). A mug or bowl is a cylinder; a drink adds a coloured cylinder inside. A donut is a torus. A pizza is a flat cylinder with small sphere/box toppings.

## Presentation

Place the food on a plate (a wide flat cylinder) or a wooden board (a thin box), itself resting on a table or surface. Group the food with its plate if they move together. Keep a clean, uncluttered surface around the item.

## Proportions

Treat 1 unit as roughly 1 metre, so food is small: a mug about 0.1m radius and 0.12m tall, a cake about 0.15-0.25m radius, a plate about 0.15m radius. Stack layers so each sits exactly on the one below, and rest the plate on the surface.

## Materials and lighting

Use warm, appetising, saturated colours with medium-high roughness and no metalness (food is not shiny, except a faint sheen on glaze or sauce via lower roughness). Light softly and warmly — a soft key plus fill — to make the food look fresh. A shallow depth, close camera framing reads as a food shot.

## Common failure modes

Food at the wrong scale (a metre-wide burger) — keep items small relative to the plate and table. Floating toppings or layers — stack each piece on the one below. A shiny metallic look — keep roughness high.
